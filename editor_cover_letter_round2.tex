\begin{letter}{Dr.\ Zhihong Tian\\Editorial Board Member\\Scientific Reports}

\opening{Dear Dr.\ Tian,}

Thank you for inviting a revision of our manuscript,
\emph{Dimensionality reduction for homological stability and global structure
preservation}
(submission 76c16c04-103b-46e3-b1ba-f3842ac73254).

We have made a substantial evidence-based revision while retaining the exact
submitted title and the manuscript's pre--Reviewer~3 general framing. DiRe is a
dimensionality-reduction framework for a user-specified target dimension $d$,
including non-visual dimensions used in subsequent analysis. The value $d=2$
describes the visual benchmark configuration in this paper; it does not
redefine the method. We therefore did not accept Reviewer~3's proposed
visualization-only framing. The revision also corrects its discussion of
initialization and modern tSNE, delineates DiRe from the broader
attraction--repulsion family, states the sampled layout algorithm and
complexity, engages prior dimensionality-reduction evaluation literature,
separates CPU and GPU timing panels, and adds PCA and openTSNE references.

Most significantly, we added full public tests on 1,306,127 10x mouse-brain
cells and 723,457 arXiv paper embeddings. A version-pinned push-button suite
records end-to-end cold and steady timings, peak incremental GPU-memory use,
parameters, versions, fixed diagnostic row-index subsets, metrics, and
failures. A second command
retrieves a compact checksummed bundle and regenerates all figures and tables
locally without a GPU.

The new results establish a concrete reason for DiRe's existence without
claiming universal superiority. In the six-dataset direct-atlas audit, default
DiRe has lower mean topology discrepancy than cuML UMAP in
\SmallAtlasDireLowerVsUmapCount{} of
\SmallAtlasUmapComparisonCount{} dataset--homology comparisons; in
\SmallAtlasDireFivePctLowerVsUmapCount{} the relative gap exceeds five
percent. The tSNE comparison splits
\SmallAtlasDireLowerVsTsneCount{}--\SmallAtlasTsneLowerVsDireCount{},
including tSNE's strength on MNIST and DiRe's strength on Disk and
Half--moons. At full scale, production-policy DiRe is the fastest nonlinear
GPU method on both public datasets. On arXiv it also reduces mean
unit-diameter Betti--DTW discrepancy relative to UMAP and tSNE by
\ArxivDireBetaZeroDtwReductionVsUmapPct\% and
\ArxivDireBetaZeroDtwReductionVsTsnePct\% in $H_0$, and by
\ArxivDireBetaOneDtwReductionVsUmapPct\% and
\ArxivDireBetaOneDtwReductionVsTsnePct\% in $H_1$. The manuscript reports the
counter-results and normalization sensitivity equally explicitly.

The topology analysis now reports exact diameter-normalised bottleneck
distances alongside the retained Betti--DTW score and a separately labelled
robust-scale sensitivity calculation. Default DiRe is separated from a
spectral-initialisation control and the source-predeclared topology preset.
DiRe and every newly executed baseline are fitted to all observations; the
separately labelled Cell Ranger tSNE is a released full-cell reference, not a
same-input or runtime baseline. Only the intrinsically combinatorial
persistence diagnostic uses controlled fixed, shared row--index subsets. Those coordinates
and diagrams are included in the bundle, and a CPU audit command independently
recomputes every underlying topology score. We executed that audit with no GPU
exposed: all \TopologyAuditComparisonCount{} recomputed scalar scores matched
the packaged values, with maximum absolute difference
\TopologyAuditMaximumAbsoluteDelta{}. The revised protocol uses
\TopologySubsetCount{} independently seeded, fixed 4,000--row index subsets,
each applied identically to the high--dimensional input and every embedding.
Three independently seeded full layouts are evaluated separately on one fixed
subset. It also adds nested $m\in\{1000,2000,4000\}$ checks and paired
effect-size/variability records, without converting the descriptive subset
audit into a conventional significance claim.

The production DiRe benchmark uses the package's automatic cuVS policy. A
separate fresh forced-IVF--Flat control records the effective index,
synchronized graph/initialisation/layout times, force-fallback status, and
fixed-query graph overlap without overwriting the earlier run. Runtime claims
are made only alongside the full local/global/context/topology comparison.

We have answered Reviewer~3 point by point and accepted every supportable
request. We also identify, with code and measured records, where that report is
incorrect or overstates what the evidence establishes. In particular, default
DiRe does not compute all-pairs repulsion; its fixed-parameter layout is linear
in the number of points. The report's contrary statement is a central factual
premise of its rejection recommendation and cannot survive inspection of the
released implementation.

We ask you also to consider the review sequence. Reviewer~2 had raised
overlapping concerns in the prior round and now states that those comments were
``adequately addressed.'' Introducing a new rejection recommendation on
substantially overlapping grounds after that revision created considerable
additional work. We respectfully ask the journal to avoid what would amount to
the shameful practice of moving the goalposts by repeatedly resetting matters
already addressed and accepted whenever a new reviewer is introduced. We have
nevertheless responded with stronger experiments rather than procedural
objection alone. We request that the present revision be assessed on its
actual code, stated claims, and measured evidence.
